Learn as you go

Short, plain explanations for things that come up often. Open one whenever you like — nothing to remember in advance.

An OTP (One-Time Password) is a short code sent to your phone by your bank or an app, to prove it's really you. It is meant for you alone.

A simple rule: your bank, a shop, or a delivery service will never call and ask you to tell them your OTP. If anyone asks for it over the phone, it's safe to say no and hang up.

Open WhatsApp, tap the person's name, then tap the small video camera icon at the top right. It starts ringing on their phone right away — no need to type anything.

If you'd like, Sathi can add a large "Video call Aarav" button to your home screen so you don't need to search for it each time.

Be cautious if a caller creates urgency ("act now or your account will be blocked"), asks for money, an OTP, or your card PIN, or claims to be from the police, a bank, or a courier with a problem only you can fix by paying.

It's always alright to hang up and call a family member or the bank's official number yourself before doing anything.

Open Google Pay or PhonePe, tap "Bills", choose your electricity board, and enter your consumer number (found on last month's paper bill). It will show the amount due — check it matches your bill, then confirm with your UPI PIN.
